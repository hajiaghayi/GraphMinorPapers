\textbf{Proof.} We proceed by induction on $\Sigma$, and assume inductively that the result holds for every surface with null boundary simpler than $\Sigma$. Consequently,

\hspace*{12pt}(1) \textit{For every $\phi \ge 1$ there exists $f(\phi) \ge \phi$ such that for every surface $\Sigma'$ with $bd(\Sigma') = \emptyset$ simpler than $\Sigma$, if $\Gamma$ is a drawing in $\Sigma'$ with a tangle of order $\ge f(\phi)$, and $\Gamma'$ is obtained from $\Gamma$ by deleting some vertices and edges, all incident with one region, then $\Gamma'$ has a tangle of order $\ge \phi$.}

Given $\theta' \ge 1$, let $\theta = \theta' + 6 + f(f(\theta'))$; we shall show that $\theta$ satisfies the theorem. Let $\Gamma$ be a drawing in $\Sigma$, let $\mathcal{T}$ be a tangle in $\Gamma$ of order $\ge \theta$, let $r$ be a region of $\Gamma$ in $\Sigma$, and let $\Gamma'$ be a drawing obtained from $\Gamma$ by deleting some vertices and edges of $\Gamma$ all incident with $r$.

There exists $(\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2) \in \mathcal{T}$ of order $0$ such that $\Gamma_2$ is connected, by theorem (2.8) of [6] applied to the $1$-truncation of $\mathcal{T}$. By (3.3) there is a tangle in $\Gamma_2$ of order $\ge \theta$, and $\Gamma' \cap \Gamma_2$ is obtained from $\Gamma_2$ by deleting some vertices and edges all on one region of $\Gamma_2$ in $\Sigma$. If $\Gamma' \cap \Gamma_2$ has a tangle of order $\ge \theta'$, then so does $\Gamma'$ by (3.4). Consequently it suffices to prove the theorem for $\Gamma_2$; in other words, we may assume that $\Gamma$ is connected.

Let $\mathcal{T}_1$ be the $(\theta' + 6)$-truncation of $\mathcal{T}$. If $\mathcal{T}_1$ is respectful, then by theorem \del{(7.8) of [7] (with $k = 3$)}\fix{(7.5) of [7], applied to the region $r$, there is a 2-cell subdrawing of $\Gamma$ with no vertex incident with $r$ and with a respectful tangle of order $\theta' + 4$; by (3.7) it has a tangle of order $\theta'$, and since it is a subdrawing of $\Gamma'$, by (3.4)}, there is a tangle in $\Gamma'$ of order $\theta'$\del{, since $\theta' + 6 \ge 9$}, as required.\fixnote{fix:4.5-proof}{As printed, the step uses theorem (7.8) of [7] (Graph Minors XII, page 268). That theorem asks for a 2-cell subdrawing, and $\Gamma'$ need not be 2-cell: if a vertex $u$ of degree 1 has its edge in the closure of $r$, deleting the edge but not $u$ leaves $u$ alone in a region that is not an open disc. Also, (7.8) with $k = 3$ needs a tangle of order at least 8, and $\theta' + 6 = 7$ when $\theta' = 1$. The rest of the proof and the bound $\theta = \theta' + 6 + f(f(\theta'))$ are unchanged.} We may assume therefore that $\mathcal{T}_1$ is not respectful. Since $\Gamma$ is connected, we deduce

\hspace*{12pt}(2) \textit{There is a $\Gamma$-normal $O$-arc $F \subseteq \Sigma$ with $|F \cap V(\Gamma)| < \theta' + 6$, such that there is no closed disc $\Delta \subseteq \Sigma$ bounded by $F$ with $(\Gamma \cap \Delta, \overline{\Gamma \cap \Sigma \setminus \Delta}) \in \mathcal{T}$.}

It follows that $\Sigma$ is not a sphere. There are two cases, depending on whether $F$ separates $\Sigma$ or not. We assume first that it does not. Let $\Sigma'$ be obtained from $\Sigma$ by cutting along $F$. Then $\hat{\Sigma}'$ is simpler than $\Sigma$ by (4.1)(iii). Let $\Gamma''$ be obtained from $\Gamma$ by deleting all vertices in $F \cap V(\Gamma)$. By (3.2), $\Gamma''$ has a tangle of order
\begin{equation*}
ord(\mathcal{T}) - |F \cap V(\Gamma)| \ge \theta - (\theta' + 6) = f(f(\theta')).
\end{equation*}
Now $\Gamma''$ is a drawing in $\hat{\Sigma}'$ and there are one or two regions of $\Gamma''$ in $\hat{\Sigma}'$, say $r_1$ and $r_2$ where possibly $r_1 = r_2$, such that $\Gamma' \cap \Gamma''$ is obtained from $\Gamma''$ by deleting some vertices and edges incident with either $r_1$ or $r_2$. By two applications of (1), we deduce that $\Gamma' \cap \Gamma''$ has a tangle of order $\ge \theta'$, and hence so does $\Gamma'$ by (3.4), as required.
