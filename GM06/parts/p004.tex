Put $\Gamma = \Delta \cup \Delta^*$ and $W = V \cup U$.

\stmt
(2.3) \textit{If} $W \ne \emptyset$, \textit{one of} $\Delta, \Delta^*$ \textit{has a member with cardinality} $1$.

\textit{Proof.} Choose $c \in C - W$, and let $W = \{w_1, \dots, w_{2k}\}$, numbered in order on $C - \{c\}$. Choose $i, j$ with $1 \le i \le j \le 2k$ so that

\hspace*{12pt}(i) for some $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $\{x, c\}$ crosses $\{w_i, w_j\}$ for all $x \in \gamma - \{w_i, w_j\}$

\hspace*{12pt}(ii) subject to (i), $j - i$ is minimum.

(This is possible since the choice $i = 1$, $j = 2k$ satisfies (i), because $k \ge 1$.) If $j = i$ the theorem is true. Suppose for a contradiction that $j > i$, and choose $\gamma$ satisfying (i). Now $w_i, w_j \in \gamma$ by the minimality of $j - i$, and so $j - i$ is even and hence $j - i \ge 2$. Choose $\gamma' \in \Gamma$ with $w_{i+1} \in \gamma'$. Now if $x \in \gamma'$ then $x \ne w_j$, and $\{w_{i+1}, x\}$ does not cross $\{w_i, w_j\}$ by definition of $\Delta^*$; thus either $x = w_{i+1}$ or $\{x, c\}$ crosses $\{w_{i+1}, w_j\}$. This contradicts the minimality of $j - i$, as required.
