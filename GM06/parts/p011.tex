\stmt
(4.2) \textit{If $\Sigma$ is a cylinder and neither (4.1) nor reduction 1 nor reduction 2 applies, then each component of $H$ has exactly one vertex in each cuff of $\Sigma$.}

For the proof we need a lemma. Let $C$ be a cuff of a cylinder $\Sigma$, and let $S \subset C$ be connected. For distinct $a, b \in S$ we denote by $S(a, b)$ the component of $C - \{a, b\}$ which is contained in $S$.

\stmt
(4.3) \textit{Let $\Sigma$ be a cylinder, and let $C$ be a cuff of $\Sigma$. Let $s, t \in C$ be distinct, and let $S$ be a component of $C - \{s, t\}$. Let $K$ be a connected graph with $|V(K)| \ge 2$, properly drawn on $\Sigma$ with $V(K) \subseteq S$. Then there is an edge of $K$ with distinct ends $a, b$ say, such that $S(a, b) \cap V(K) = \emptyset$.}

\textit{Proof.} Choose distinct $a, b \in S$, adjacent in $K$, so that $S(a, b)$ is minimal. Suppose that $S(a, b) \cap V(K) \ne \emptyset$, and choose
\begin{equation*}
c \in S(a, b) \cap V(K).
\end{equation*}
Since $K$ is connected, there is a vertex $d \ne c$ adjacent in $K$ to $c$, and then $d \in S(a, b) \cup \{a, b\}$. Hence $S(c, d) \subset S(a, b)$, a contradiction as required.
