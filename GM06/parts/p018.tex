\textit{Proof of} (5.3). We proceed by induction on $|V(G)| + |E(G)|$. If reduction 1, 2 or 3 applies to $(G, \mathscr{L})$ then the result follows from our inductive hypothesis and (5.4), (5.5). If reduction 4 applies and results in $(G', \mathscr{L}')$ say, then as we observed before one of reductions 1, 2 or 3 applies to $(G', \mathscr{L}')$ and hence the theorem is true for $(G', \mathscr{L}')$; and again by (5.4), (5.5) it is true for $(G, \mathscr{L})$. If none of reductions 1,..., 4 applies, then by (5.1) some $s_i$ has valency 1 and its neighbor $t$ say is in $C_2$, since $\theta(\mathscr{L}) + 1/k$ is not feasible. Let $e$ be the edge incident with $s_i$; then $P_i$ consists solely of the edge $e$. Let $g \colon [0, 1] \to \Sigma$ be continuous, with
\begin{align*}
g(0) &= \left( 1, \frac{2i+1}{k} \pi \right), \qquad g(1) = t, \\
g(x) &\in R_i \qquad (0 \le x < 1).
\end{align*}
Now
\begin{equation*}
t = \left( 2, \left( \frac{2i}{k} + 2\theta(\mathscr{L}) \right) \pi \right).
\end{equation*}
