\textbf{5.6.} \textit{Let $P_1, P_2$ be partial $\Omega$-patchworks, and let $(A_1, B_1)$, $(A_2, B_2)$ be separations of $\|P_1\|, \|P_2\|$ respectively. Let $\eta'$ be a realizable expansion of $P_1|A_1$ in $P_2|A_2$ (whence $|\bar{\pi}(A_1)| = |\bar{\pi}(A_2)| = k$, say) and let $\eta''$ be a realizable expansion of $P_1|B_1$ in $P_2|B_2$ such that for $1 \le i \le k$, $\eta''(v)$ contains the $i$th term of $\pi(A_2)$, where $v$ is the $i$th term of $\pi(A_1)$. Define $\eta$ by:}
\begin{equation*}
\eta(v) = \begin{cases}
\eta'(v), & v \in V(A_1) \setminus V(B_1), \\
\eta''(v), & v \in V(B_1) \setminus V(A_1), \\
\eta'(v) \cup \eta''(v), & v \in V(A_1 \cap B_1),
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\eta(e) = \begin{cases}
\eta'(e), & e \in E(A_1), \\
\eta''(e), & e \in E(B_1).
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
\textit{Then $\eta$ is a realizable expansion of $P_1$ in $P_2$.}

\textbf{Proof.} Let $*$ be a new element and let $\Omega'$ be the well-quasi-order with $\Omega \subseteq \Omega'$ and $E(\Omega') = E(\Omega) \cup \{*\}$, in which $* < x$ for all $x \in E(\Omega)$. For $i = 1, 2$, let $P_i = (G_i, \mu_i, \Delta_i, \phi_i)$; and for all $e \in E(G_i)$, define $\phi_i'(e) = \phi_i(e)$ if $e \in dom(\phi_i)$, and otherwise $\phi_i'(e) = *$. Let $P_i' = (G_i, \mu_i, \Delta_i, \phi_i')$; then $P_i'$ is an $\Omega'$-patchwork. Since $\eta'$ is a realizable expansion of $P_1|A_1$ in $P_2|A_2$, it follows that it is also a realizable expansion of $P_1'|A_1$ in $P_2'|A_2$. (Here we use that $* \le x$ for all $x \in E(\Omega)$.) Similarly, $\eta''$ is a realizable expansion of $P_1'|B_1$ in $P_2'|B_2$.

By Theorem 8.1 of [2] applied to these two $\Omega'$-patchworks, we deduce that $\eta$ is a realizable expansion of $P_1'$ in $P_2'$. For each $e \in dom(\phi_1)$, it follows that $\phi_2(\eta(e)) \ne *$, and therefore $\eta(e) \in dom(\phi_2)$; and consequently $\eta$ is a realizable expansion of $P_1$ in $P_2$. This proves 5.6. $\square$

If $P = (G, \mu, \Delta, \phi)$ is an $\Omega$-patchwork, we write $V(P) = V(G), E(P) = E(G)$.
