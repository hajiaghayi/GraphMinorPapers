\textit{Proof.} Let $(G, T) \in \mathscr{B}(k, r, s)$, and let $(\Gamma, F_1, F_2)$ be a framing of $(G, T)$ in $\Sigma$ which is not $(r, s)$-boundary-linked. Let $|V(\Gamma) \cap F_i| = k_i$ $(i = 1, 2)$. We assume $k_1 \ge k_2$ without loss of generality; and certainly $k_1 + k_2 \le 2k$. Let $k_3$ be the minimum value of $|V(\Gamma) \cap F|$ taken over all $\Gamma$-normal hoops $F \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F_2)$. Then $k_2 \ge k_3$ since $F_2$ is a possible choice of $F$. Since $k_1 \ge k_2 \ge k_3$, one of the following three cases must occur.

\textit{Case} 1. $k_1 = k_2 = k_3$. In this case we claim that $(G, T) \in \mathscr{A}(k, r, s)$. For suppose that $F_1', F_2'$ are $\Gamma$-normal laminar hoops with $F_1', F_2' \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F_2)$, and $(\Gamma \cap \Sigma(F_1', F_2'), F_1', F_2')$ has width $\ge r$ and length $\ge s$. We assume without loss of generality that $F_2' \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F_1')$ and $F_1' \subseteq \Sigma(F_2, F_2')$. But by Menger's theorem there are $k_3$ mutually vertex-disjoint paths of $\Gamma$ between $F_i$ and $F_i'$ $(i = 1, 2)$, and so $(\Gamma, F_1, F_2)$ is $(r, s)$-boundary-linked, contrary to our hypothesis. Thus such $F_1', F_2'$ do not exist, and so $(G, T) \in \mathscr{A}(k, r, s)$ as claimed.

\textit{Case} 2. $k_1 > k_2 = k_3$. In this case we claim that $(G, T) \in W(\mathscr{B}(k - 1) \oplus \mathscr{A}(2k, r, s + 2))$. For let $F$ be a $\Gamma$-normal hoop with $F \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F_2)$ and with $|V(\Gamma) \cap F| < k_1$, chosen so that the side of $F$ including $F_1 - F$ contains as few vertices of $\Gamma$ as possible. Let $\Gamma_i = \Gamma \cap \Sigma(F, F_i)$ and let $(\Gamma_i, F, F_i)$ be a framing of some graft $(G_i, T_i)$ $(i = 1, 2)$.

Now $(G_2, T_2) \in \mathscr{B}(k - 1)$ since $|V(\Gamma) \cap F| < k_1$. We claim that $(G_1, T_1) \in \mathscr{A}(2k, r, s + 2)$. Certainly $|T_1| \le k_1 + k_3 \le 2k$. Suppose then that $F_1', F_2' \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F)$ are $\Gamma$-normal laminar hoops, and $(\Gamma', F_1', F_2')$ has width $\ge r$ and length $\ge s + 2 \ge \max(s + 1, 2)$, where $\Gamma' = \Gamma \cap \Sigma(F_1', F_2')$. We assume without loss of generality that $F_1' \subseteq \Sigma(F_2, F_2')$, $F_2' \subseteq \Sigma(F_1, F_1')$. It is easy to see that there is a $\Gamma$-normal hoop $F_1'' \subseteq \Sigma(F_1', F_2')$, such that the tube $(\Gamma'', F_1'', F_2')$ has length $\ge s$, where $\Gamma'' = \Gamma \cap \Sigma(F_1'', F_2')$, and such that $F_1'' \cap \Gamma \nsubseteq F_1' \cap \Gamma$. Then $(\Gamma'', F_1'', F_2')$ has width $\ge r$ and length $\ge s$. Moreover, there are $k_2$ mutually disjoint paths of $\Gamma$ between $F_1$ and $F_2$, since $k_3 \ge k_2$, and hence there are $k_2$ mutually disjoint paths of $\Gamma$ between $F_2''$ and $F_2$. Also, from the choice of $F$, there are $k_1$ mutually disjoint paths of $\Gamma$ between $F_1''$ and $F_1$. Thus $(\Gamma, F_1, F_2)$ is $(r, s)$-boundary-linked, a contradiction. Hence there do not exist such $F_1', F_2'$ and so $(G_1, T_1) \in \mathscr{A}(2k, r, s + 2)$ as claimed. It follows that
\begin{equation*}
(G, T) \in W(\mathscr{B}(k - 1) \oplus \mathscr{A}(2k, r, s + 2)).
\end{equation*}
