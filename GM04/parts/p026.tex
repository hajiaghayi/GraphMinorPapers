Axiom 4. Let $n \ge 0$; we must show that $\mathscr{S}_n$ is good. Certainly $\sigma \times S \in \mathscr{R}$ for each $(S, \sigma) \in \mathscr{S}_n$, and $\mathscr{S}_n$ has index $\le n$, by definition of $\mathscr{S}_n$. Let $(S_i, \sigma_i)$ $(i = 1, 2, \dots)$ be a countable sequence of members of $\mathscr{S}_n$ such that the set of all tips of all the $(S_i, \sigma_i)$'s is well-simulated. Let $G_i = \sigma_i \times S_i$ $(i \ge 1)$. It remains to show that there exist $i' > i \ge 1$ such that $G_i$ is simulated in $G_{i'}$, that is, $P(G_i)$ is simulated in $P(G_{i'})$. Since $P_1, P_2, \dots$ are all robust, it follows that $P(G)$ is robust for all $G \in \mathscr{R}$, and in particular each $P(G_i)$ is removable. Now for each $i \ge 1$, $(S_i, \sigma_i)$ is a star-decomposition of $G_i$ with $|V(\sigma_i(o(S_i)))| \le n$; and $\{P(G_i) \mid \sigma_i(s) \colon i \ge 1, s \in U(S_i)\}$ is well-quasi-ordered by simulation, since the set of all tips of all the $(S_i, \sigma_i)$'s is well-simulated. From (8.4), it follows that there exist $i' > i \ge 1$ such that $P(G_i)$ is simulated in $P(G_{i'})$. This verifies Axiom 4.
