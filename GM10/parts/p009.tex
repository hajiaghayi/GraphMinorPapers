\alpha'(t_1, e_1) &= \alpha(t_1, e_1) \cup A_2 = E - (A_1 - A_2) \\

We claim that $\kappa(A_1 - A_2) \ge \kappa(A_1)$. For if $\kappa(A_1 - A_2) \ge 0$ this is true, and so we may assume that $A_1 - A_2$ is efficient. Then $\alpha'$ is a tree-labelling over $\mathscr{A}$, and from (2),
\begin{equation*}
\kappa(\alpha'(t, e_1)) + \kappa(\alpha'(t_1, e_1)) \ge \kappa(\alpha(t, e_1)) + \kappa(\alpha(t_1, e_1));
\end{equation*}
that is,
\begin{equation*}
\kappa(A_1 - A_2) + \kappa(E - (A_1 - A_2)) \ge \kappa(A_1) + \kappa(E - A_1).
\end{equation*}
Since $\kappa(E - (A_1 - A_2)) = \kappa(A_1 - A_2)$ and $\kappa(E - A_1) = \kappa(A_1)$, it follows that $\kappa(A_1 - A_2) \ge \kappa(A_1)$, as claimed. Similarly $\kappa(A_2 - A_1) \ge \kappa(A_2)$. But since $\kappa$ is a connectivity function,
\begin{equation*}
\kappa(A_1) + \kappa(E - A_2) \ge \kappa(A_1 \cup (E - A_2)) + \kappa(A_1 \cap (E - A_2));
\end{equation*}
that is,
\begin{equation*}
\kappa(A_1) + \kappa(A_2) \ge \kappa(A_2 - A_1) + \kappa(A_1 - A_2).
\end{equation*}
Thus equality holds throughout, and in particular, $\kappa(A_1 - A_2) = \kappa(A_1)$ and $\kappa(A_2 - A_1) = \kappa(A_2)$. From the symmetry between $t_1$ and $t_2$, we may assume that $d(t) < d(t_1)$. With $\alpha'$ as before we see that $\alpha'$ is a tree-labelling over $\mathscr{A}$ and $\sum \kappa(\alpha'(v, e)) = \sum \kappa(\alpha(v, e))$. Moreover, $\{e_1, e_2\}$ is exact for $\alpha'$, and any fork of $T$ which is exact for $\alpha$ is exact for $\alpha'$ except possibly for forks $\{e, e_1\}$ with nub $t_1$. There are at most two such forks, and since $d(t_1) > d(t)$, this contradicts (3), as required. $\square$
