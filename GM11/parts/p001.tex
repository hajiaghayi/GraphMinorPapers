\begin{quote}
Let $\Gamma$ be a graph or hypergraph drawn on a connected surface which is not a sphere, in such a way that every non-null-homotopic curve meets the drawing at least $\theta$ times. We show that this defines a ``tangle of order $\theta$'' in $\Gamma$, in the sense of earlier papers of this series. Also, there is a natural distance function defined by the drawing, and we show that for any point $c$ of the surface, and any $\theta' < \theta$, the set of points within distance $\theta'$ of $c$ is simply-connected.

\end{quote}

\sectionhead{1. Introduction}

This is one of a series of papers establishing lemmas towards the proof of a theorem concerning the structure imposed on graphs by the exclusion of a fixed minor. The main proof of that theorem, which will appear in a later paper, involves among other things a long and cumbersome argument about graphs drawn on connected surfaces. Unfortunately, the case when the surface is a sphere is in some ways different from (and more difficult than) the higher genus case. It would be a pity to have to treat the two cases separately, because large parts of the arguments involved are identical, and the purpose of this paper is to develop a framework within which both cases can be handled simultaneously.

If the surface is not a sphere, and the drawing is ''$\theta$-representative,'' that is, every non-null-homotopic closed curve meets the drawing at least $\theta$ times, then any simple closed curve which meets the drawing $< \theta$ times bounds a unique disc, which we may call its ``inside.''
