\sectionhead{5. The Main Result---Second Version}

The preliminary form (4.6) of our main result needs refinement. The hypotheses that $c \ge 2$ and that $|V(G) \cap C| \ge 2$ for each cuff $C$ are unnatural, and were introduced for technical reasons---we shall show later how to remove them. But the principal defect of (4.6) is hypothesis (ii), which is too strong. In this section we replace it with a weaker condition.

A subset $X \subseteq \Sigma$ is \textit{planar} if $X \subseteq \Delta$ for some closed disc $\Delta \subseteq \Sigma$. Thus an $O$-arc is planar if and only if it is null-homotopic. Let us say that $X \subseteq \Sigma$ is \textit{solid} if $X$ is closed and locally arc-connected (see Section 11). Thus, for example, if $G$ is a proper graph in $\Sigma$, the closure of the union of some of the regions and some of the edges of $G$ is solid.

We shall assume the following fact (implied by (11.2) and (11.10)).

\stmt
(5.1) \textit{If $\Sigma$ is connected and $X \subseteq \Sigma$ is solid and $X \ne \Sigma$, then $X$ is planar if and only if every $O$-arc included in $X$ is null-homotopic.}

We say that $X \subseteq \Sigma$ is \textit{near-planar} if either $X$ is planar, or $X$ is planar in $\Sigma + \hat{C}$, for some cuff $C$. We say that $X$ \textit{surrounds} cuff $C$ (in $\Sigma$) if $X$ is planar in $\Sigma + \hat{C}$ but not in $\Sigma$. It follows easily from (5.1) that

\stmt
(5.2) \textit{If $X \subseteq \Sigma$ is solid and $X$ is planar in $\Sigma + \hat{C}$, where $C$ is a cuff, then $X$ surrounds $C$ in $\Sigma$ if and only if some $O$-arc included in $X$ surrounds $C$.}

Let $G$ be a proper graph in $\Sigma$. We define $\alpha(G)$ to be the minimum of $|X \cap V(G)|$, taken over all solid connected $G$-normal sets $X \subseteq \Sigma$ which are not near-planar. (It is easy to see that the minimum exists provided that $\Sigma \not\cong \Sigma(0, 0, c)$ for $c = 0$, $1$, or $2$.)
