\textit{Proof of} (5.5). The ``only if'' part of (5.5) is clear. For the ``if'' part, let $Z$ be as in (5.5), such that every $G$-normal $O$-arc included in $Z$ is null-homotopic. Since $Z$ is the closure of the union of some of the regions of $G$, it follows that $Z$ is solid. The result follows from (11.10) if every continuous map $\phi \colon S^1 \to Z$ is null-homotopic. Let $\phi \colon S^1 \to Z$ be continuous. For each loop $e$ of $G$, choose a point $v_e \in e$, and let $G'$ be the graph with
\begin{align*}
U(G') &= U(G) \\
V(G') &= V(G) \cup \{v_e \colon e \text{ is a loop of } G\}.
\end{align*}
Then $G'$ has no loops. For each $e \in E(G')$, since $e$ is not a loop, we may choose a closed disc $\Delta_e \supseteq \bar{e}$, with the ends of $e$ in $\operatorname{bd}(\Delta_e)$, with $e \subseteq \Delta_e - \operatorname{bd}(\Delta_e)$ and with $\Delta_e \cap U(G') = \bar{e}$; and moreover, we may make these choices so that $\Delta_e \cap \Delta_f \subseteq \bar{e} \cap \bar{f}$ for all distinct $e, f \in E(G')$. For each $e \in E(G')$, either $Z \cap \operatorname{bd}(\Delta_e)$ is arc-connected or it consists just of the ends of $e$; and so if $|\phi| \cap e \ne \emptyset$ then $Z \cap \operatorname{bd}(\Delta_e)$ is arc-connected. By applying (11.3) to each $\Delta_e$ with $|\phi| \cap e \ne \emptyset$, we deduce that $\phi$ is homotopic to a map $\psi' \colon S^1 \to Z$ with $|\psi'|$ $G'$-normal. It is easy to see that $\psi'$ is homotopic to a map $\psi \colon S^1 \to Z$ with $|\psi|$ $G$-normal. By (11.2), $\psi$ is null-homotopic, and hence so is $\phi$, as required.
