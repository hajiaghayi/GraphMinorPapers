\begin{quote}
Let $\Gamma$ be a graph drawn on a connected surface $\Sigma$ which is not a sphere. It is ``$\theta$-representative'' if every non-null-homotopic closed curve meets $\Gamma$ at least $\theta$ times. Also, $\Gamma$ defines a metric on $\Sigma$, discussed in an earlier paper. Our objective here is to study the effect on the metric and on the ``representativeness'' of making local changes in the drawing or in the surface. We also reformulate more compactly the main theorem of an earlier paper in terms of this metric. These are lemmas to be used later.
\end{quote}

\sectionhead{1. Introduction}

In future papers of this series we shall prove a theorem about the structure of the graphs not containing a fixed graph as a minor. Our objective here is to establish certain lemmas towards that proof, concerning drawings of graphs in surfaces, and the associated metric, introduced in [5].

A surface is a compact 2-manifold, without boundary. Let $\Sigma$ be a surface. A line in $\Sigma$ is a subset homeomorphic to $[0, 1]$, and its ends are defined in the natural way. An $O$-arc is a subset homeomorphic to a circle. If $\Delta \subseteq \Sigma$, $\bar{\Delta}$ denotes the closure of $\Delta$, and $bd(\Delta)$ denotes $\bar{\Delta} \cap \overline{\Sigma - \Delta}$. A drawing in $\Sigma$ is a pair $(U, V)$, where $V \subseteq U \subseteq \Sigma$, $U$ is closed, $V$ is finite, $U - V$ has only finitely many arc-wise connected components, called \textit{edges}, and for every edge $e$, either $\bar{e}$ is a line with set of ends $\bar{e} \cap V$, or $\bar{e}$ is an $O$-arc and $|\bar{e} \cap V| = 1$.
