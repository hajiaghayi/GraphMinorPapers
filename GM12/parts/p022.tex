\stmt
(7.3) \textit{Let $\mathscr{T}$ be a respectful tangle of order $\theta \ge 2$ in a 2-cell drawing $\Gamma$ in a connected surface $\Sigma$, and let its metric be $d$. Let $v \in V(\Gamma)$, such that $\Gamma \setminus v$ is 2-cell. Then there is a respectful tangle $\mathscr{T}'$ of order $\theta - 1$ in $\Gamma \setminus v$, with metric $d'$, such that if $r'$ is the region of $\Gamma \setminus v$ containing $v$, then}

\hspace*{12pt}(i) \textit{$d'(a, b) \ge d(a, b) - 2$ for all $a, b \in A(\Gamma) \cap A(\Gamma \setminus v)$, and}

\hspace*{12pt}(ii) \textit{$d'(a, r') \ge d(a, \{v\}) - 1$ for all $a \in A(\Gamma) \cap A(\Gamma \setminus v)$.}

\textit{Proof.} Let $\Gamma' = \Gamma \setminus v$, and let
\begin{equation*}
\mathscr{T}' = \{(A \setminus v, B \setminus v) : (A, B) \in \mathscr{T}, v \in V(A \cap B)\}.
\end{equation*}
By [4, Theorem (8.5)], $\mathscr{T}'$ is a tangle of order $\theta - 1$ in $\Gamma'$. Let us prove that it is respectful. We may therefore assume that $\Sigma$ is not a sphere, for every tangle in a drawing in a sphere is respectful. Hence $\Gamma$ is $\theta$-representative, and so $\Gamma'$ is $(\theta - 1)$-representative. Let $F$ be a $\Gamma'$-normal $O$-arc with $|F \cap V(\Gamma')| < \theta - 1$. Since $\Gamma'$ is $(\theta - 1)$-representative, there is a closed disc $\Delta \subseteq \Sigma$ bounded by $F$. Let $A' = \Gamma' \cap \Delta$, $B' = \Gamma' \cap \overline{\Sigma - \Delta}$; then $(A', B')$ is a separation of $\Gamma'$ of order $< \theta - 1$. Hence one of $(A', B')$, $(B', A')$ belongs to $\mathscr{T}'$, and we assume for a contradiction that $(B', A') \in \mathscr{T}'$. Therefore there exists $(A, B) \in \mathscr{T}$ with $v \in V(A \cap B)$ and $A \setminus v = B'$, $B \setminus v = A'$. Since $\mathscr{T}$ is respectful, by [5, Theorem 6.4)] there is a circuit $C$ of $B$ with $V(A \cap C) = \emptyset$, such that $U(C)$ is non-null-homotopic in $\Sigma$. Since $v \in V(A)$ and hence $v \notin V(C)$, it follows that $C \subseteq B \setminus v = A'$ and so $U(C) \subseteq U(A') \subseteq \Delta$, a contradiction since $U(C)$ is non-null-homotopic. Hence $(A', B') \in \mathscr{T}'$, and so $\mathscr{T}'$ is respectful. Let $\operatorname{ins}'$ be the corresponding ``inside'' function.

To prove (ii), let $K$ be a radial drawing of $\Gamma$. Now no two edges of $K$ incident with $v$ are parallel, since $\Gamma \setminus v$ is 2-cell.\fixnote{fix:7.3-proof}{This is false when $\Gamma$ has a loop at $v$ that bounds a disc. For example, take $K_4$ in the sphere, put a new vertex $v$ in the middle of one edge, and add at $v$ a loop $e$ that bounds an empty disc inside a region $r_2$ at $v$. Then $\Gamma \setminus v$ is 2-cell, but $r_2$ meets $v$ at two corners, on the two sides of $e$, so $K$ has two parallel edges between $v$ and the vertex of $K$ in $r_2$. The proof can be completed by first applying (7.2) repeatedly, to remove the inside of every $\Gamma$-normal $O$-arc through $v$ whose inside contains an edge of $\Gamma$. This removes such loops and does not decrease the metric. The rest of the argument is then as printed, and (7.3) holds as printed.} Moreover, a radial drawing of $\Gamma'$ can be obtained from $K$ by contracting all edges of $K$ incident with $v$, and then repeatedly deleting one edge from any two parallel edges that bound a region, until none remain. Consequently we may choose a radial drawing $K'$ of $\Gamma'$ with $K' \cap (\Sigma - r) = K \cap (\Sigma - r)$ and with $v \in V(K')$. Let the edges of $K'$ incident with $v$ be $e_1, \dots, e_k$ in cyclic order, where $e_i$ has ends $v, v_i$, say; then to each $e_i$ there corresponds a 2-edge path $P_i$ of $K$ with ends $v, v_i$, where in the natural sense $P_1, \dots, P_k$ are again in cyclic order and each edge of $K$ incident with a neighbour of $v$ but not incident with $v$ belongs to exactly one of $P_1, \dots, P_k$. For any closed walk $W$ of $K'$, we denote by $\widetilde{W}$ the closed walk of $K$ obtained from $W$ by replacing each occurrence of each $e_i$ by a 2-edge walk in the appropriate sense along $P_i$. Then the length of $\widetilde{W}$ is $2t$ more than the length of $W$, where $v$ occurs $t$ times in $W$.

\hspace*{12pt}(1) \textit{If $v$ occurs $t$ times in $W$ and $W$ has length $< 2\theta - 2t$, then $\operatorname{ins}'(W) \subseteq \operatorname{ins}(\widetilde{W}) \cup r$.}
