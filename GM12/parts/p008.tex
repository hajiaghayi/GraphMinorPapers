It remains to deal with the case when $\Sigma$ is a sphere and $t \le 2$. If $t = 0$ the result is vacuous. Let us assume $t = 2$. Choose $\theta'$ to satisfy (3.2) with $\Sigma$, $z$ unchanged but with $t = 3$. Let $\theta = 2\theta' + 1$. By hypothesis (i), $d(r_1, r_2) \ge \theta = 2\theta' + 1$. Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_s$ be a sequence of regions of $\Gamma$ with $a_1 = r_1$, $a_s = r_2$, such that $a_i$ and $a_{i+1}$ are both incident with some vertex for $1 \le i < s$. Then
\begin{equation*}
|d(r_1, a_{i+1}) - d(r_1, a_i)| \le d(a_i, a_{i+1}) \le 2 \quad (1 \le i < s)
\end{equation*}
and so we may choose $i$ with $1 \le i \le s$ such that $0 \le d(r_1, a_i) - \theta' \le 1$. Now
\begin{equation*}
d(r_2, a_i) \ge d(r_1, r_2) - d(r_1, a_i) \ge 2\theta' + 1 - (\theta' + 1) = \theta'.
\end{equation*}
But also $d(r_1, a_i) \ge \theta'$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
d(r_1, a_i), d(r_2, a_i), d(r_1, r_2) \ge \theta'.
\end{equation*}
Let us take $r_3 = a_i$, and $\Delta_3 \subseteq r_3$ some closed disc, and apply the theorem proved above, that is, (3.2) with $t = 3$ and $\Sigma$ a sphere. Certainly $V(\Gamma) \cap \text{bd}(\Delta_3) = \emptyset$ and, hence, is free; we deduce that the desired $H_1, \dots, H_p$ exist.

Finally, we deal with the case $t = 1$, when $\Sigma$ is a sphere. Take $\theta = z$. By [2, Theorem (3.6)] it suffices to prove that for every $\Gamma$-normal line $F_3$ with ends $a, b \in \text{bd}(\Delta_1)$ but with no other point in $\Delta_1$, we have
\begin{equation*}
|F_3 \cap V(\Gamma)| \ge \min (|F_1 \cap V(\Gamma)|, |F_2 \cap V(\Gamma)|),
\end{equation*}
where $F_1$, $F_2$ are the closures of the two components of $\text{bd}(\Delta_1) - \{a, b\}$. Suppose then that $F_3$ is such a line.

Let $F_2 \cup F_3$, $F_1 \cup F_3$ bound closed discs $D_1$, $D_2 \subseteq \Sigma$ respectively, where $D_i \cap F_i = \{a, b\}$ $(i = 1, 2)$. By the second tangle axiom, it is not the case that
\begin{equation*}
(\Gamma \cap D_i, \Gamma \cap \overline{\Sigma - D_i}) \in \mathscr{T} \quad (i = 1, 2),
\end{equation*}
since $(\Gamma \cap D_1) \cup (\Gamma \cap D_2) = \Gamma$. Thus we may assume that $(\Gamma \cap D_1, \Gamma \cap \overline{\Sigma - D_1}) \notin \mathscr{T}$.
