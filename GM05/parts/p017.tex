We come to the principal theorem of this paper.

\stmt
(7.3) \textit{If $G \in \mathcal{F}_\theta$ then $G$ has tree-width at most $\theta_9$.}

\textit{Proof.} We show, by induction on $|V(G)|$, that if $G \in \mathcal{F}_\theta$ and $X \subseteq V(G)$ and $|X| \le \theta_7 \theta_8 + 1$, then $G$ has a tree-decomposition $(T, (X_t : t \in V(T)))$ of width $\le \theta_9$, such that $X \subseteq X_t$, for some $t \in V(T)$. We assume the result holds for all graphs with fewer vertices than $G$. We may suppose that $|V(G)| > \theta_9$, for otherwise the result is clear. Hence we may assume that $|X| = \theta_7 \theta_8 + 1$. By (7.2), there is a separation $(V_1, V_2)$ of $G$ with $|V_1 \cap V_2| \le \theta_7$ and
\begin{equation*}
|(V_1 - V_2) \cap X|, |(V_2 - V_1) \cap X| \le (1 - \theta_8^{-1})|X|.
\end{equation*}
But
\begin{equation*}
|V_1 \cap V_2 \cap X| \le |V_1 \cap V_2| \le \theta_7
\end{equation*}
and so
\begin{align*}
|V_1 \cap X| &= |(V_1 - V_2) \cap X| + |V_1 \cap V_2 \cap X| \\
&\le (1 - \theta_8^{-1})|X| + \theta_7 < |X|.
\end{align*}
Hence $V_1 \ne V(G)$. Put $G_1 = G | V_1$, and
\begin{equation*}
X_1 = ((V_1 - V_2) \cap X) \cup (V_1 \cap V_2).
\end{equation*}
