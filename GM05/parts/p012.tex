Let $\mathbf{Z}^+$ denote the set of non-negative integers. If $G$ is a graph and $w \colon V(G) \to \mathbf{Z}^+$ is a function and $X \subseteq V(G)$, then $w(X)$ denotes $\sum_{v \in X} w(v)$. We use (6.1) to prove the following.

\stmt
(6.2) \textit{Let $G$ be a connected graph, let $w \colon V(G) \to \mathbf{Z}^+$ be some function, and let $k > 0$ be an integer with $k \le (w(V(G)) + 2)/3$. Then exactly one of the following is true:}

\hspace*{12pt}(i) \textit{there are disjoint subsets $V_1$, $V_2$ of $V(G)$ such that $G|V_1$, $G|V_2$ are both connected and $w(V_1), w(V_2) \ge k$;}

\hspace*{12pt}(ii) \textit{there is a vertex $v$ of $G$ such that $w(V(C)) < k$ for every component $C$ of $G \setminus v$.}

\textit{Proof.} Clearly not both (i) and (ii) hold. We prove that at least one of them holds by induction on $|V(G)|$. Suppose that $G$ is not 2-connected, and let $u$ be a vertex of $G$ such that $G \setminus u$ has more than one component. Let $V_1, ..., V_r$ be the vertex sets of the components of $G \setminus u$. If $w(V_1), ..., w(V_r) < k$ then (ii) is true. We assume then that $w(V_1) \ge k$. Define $w_1 \colon \{u\} \cup V_1 \to \mathbf{Z}^+$ by
\begin{align*}
w_1(v) &= w(v) \qquad (v \in V_1) \\
w_1(u) &= w(\{u\} \cup V_2 \cup \dots \cup V_r).
\end{align*}
Then $w_1(\{u\} \cup V_1) = w(V(G))$. Let $G_1$ be $G|(\{u\} \cup V_1)$. Now $G_1$ has fewer vertices than $G$, and so by induction the result holds for $G_1$, $w_1$.

If (i) holds for $G_1$, $w_1$ then it holds for $G$, $w$, as is easily seen. We assume then that (ii) holds for $G_1$, $w_1$, and that $v$ is a vertex of $G_1$ such that $w_1(V(C)) < k$ for every component $C$ of $G_1 \setminus v$. Now $v \ne u$, because $G_1 \setminus u$ has only one component, and its vertex set is $V_1$, and $w_1(V_1) = w(V_1) \ge k$. Hence $w(V(C)) < k$ for every component $C$ of $G \setminus v$, and (ii) is true.
