\sectionhead{4. Separating $\Omega$-Paths}

Let $(G, \Omega)$ be a society. An $\Omega$-path $P$ is \textit{non-separating} if there is an $\Omega$-path $P'$ such that $P'$ crosses $P$, and \textit{separating} otherwise. It is easy to see that $P$ is separating if and only if there is a separation $(G_1, G_2)$ of $G$ with $V(P) = V(G_1 \cap G_2)$, $\Omega(s, t) \subseteq V(G_1)$, and $\Omega(t, s) \subseteq V(G_2)$, where $P$ has ends $s, t$.

\stmt
(4.1) \textit{Let $\mathscr{P}$ be a transaction in $(G, \Omega)$, such that every peripheral member is non-separating. Then there is a crooked transaction $\mathscr{P}'$ in $(G, \Omega)$ with $|\mathscr{P}'| \ge |\mathscr{P}|$.}

\textit{Proof.} Let the number of peripheral members of $\mathscr{P}$ be $k$. If $k = 0$ the result is true, and so we assume that $k = 1$ or $2$. We shall prove there is a transaction with cardinality at least $|\mathscr{P}|$ and with $\le k - 1$ peripheral members, all non-separating, from which the result follows.

Let $(A, B)$ be a fence for $\mathscr{P}$. Choose $P \in \mathscr{P}$, peripheral in $\mathscr{P}$, and let its ends be $a \in A$, $b \in B$. Without loss of generality we assume that $\Omega(a, b) \cap V(\mathscr{P}) = \emptyset$. Now $P$ is non-separating, and so there is an $\Omega$-path $Q$ such that $Q$ crosses $P$. If $Q \cap \bigcup \mathscr{P}$ is null then $\mathscr{P} \cup \{Q\}$ is a transaction satisfying our requirement; and so we assume that $Q \cap \bigcup \mathscr{P}$ is non-null. Let $Q$ have ends $s, t$ where $s \in \Omega(a, b)$, $t \in \Omega(b, a)$. Let $Q'$ be a minimal subpath of $Q$ between $s$ and $\bigcup \mathscr{P}$. Then $Q'$ is an attachment to $\mathscr{P}$. Let its leg be $P_1$, and let the corresponding switches be $S, S'$, and let $\mathscr{P}' = (\mathscr{P} - \{P_1\}) \cup \{S\}$, $\mathscr{P}'' = \mathscr{P} - \{P_1\} \cup \{S'\}$. Both $\mathscr{P}'$ and $\mathscr{P}''$ are transactions, and so it suffices to show that in one of $\mathscr{P}', \mathscr{P}''$, every peripheral member is also peripheral in $\mathscr{P}$. Suppose not, and choose $P' \in \mathscr{P}'$ peripheral in $\mathscr{P}'$ but not in $\mathscr{P}$; and choose $P'' \in \mathscr{P}''$ similarly. Then clearly $P, P', P''$ are all distinct, and no member of $\mathscr{P} - \{P_1\}$ crosses $P'$ or $P''$. Thus $P, P', P''$ are all peripheral in the transaction $\mathscr{P} - \{P_1\}$, which is impossible. This completes the proof. $\square$
