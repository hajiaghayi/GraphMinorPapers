\textbf{8.6.} \textit{Let $\mathscr{T}$ be a tangle in $\Gamma$, and let $F_1, F_2$ be $\mathscr{T}$-enclaves. Then either $ins(F_1) \subseteq ins(F_2)$, or $ins(F_2) \subseteq ins(F_1)$, or $ins(F_1) \cap ins(F_2) = U(F_1) \cap U(F_2)$.}

\textbf{Proof.} Let $\mathscr{T}_1, \mathscr{T}_2$ be tangles such that $F_i$ is a $\mathscr{T}$-enclave around $\mathscr{T}_i$ ($i = 1,2$). Let $A_i = \Gamma \cap ins(F_i)$, $B_i = \Gamma \cap out(F_i)$ ($i = 1,2$). Since $(A_i, B_i)$ is the $(\mathscr{T}, \mathscr{T}_i)$-distinction ($i = 1,2$) it follows from Theorems 9.4 and 10.2 of [5] that one of $E(A_1 \cap A_2)$, $E(A_1 \cap B_2)$, $E(B_1 \cap A_2)$, $E(B_1 \cap B_2)$ is empty. Since $(A_i, B_i) \in \mathscr{T}$ ($i = 1,2$) it follows that $E(B_1 \cap B_2) \ne \emptyset$, and so the fourth alternative is false. From the symmetry between the second and third alternatives, we may assume without loss of generality that if either holds then the second does. Thus, one of the first two alternatives holds.

If $E(A_1 \cap B_2) = \emptyset$, then every edge of $A_1$ is included in $ins(F_2) \setminus U(F_2)$ and so every edge of $\Gamma^*$ split by $(A_1, B_1)$ is included in $ins(F_2)$; therefore $U(F_1) \subseteq ins(F_2)$, and hence $ins(F_1) \subseteq ins(F_2)$ as required. On the other hand, if $E(A_1 \cap A_2) = \emptyset$, then no edge of $\Gamma^*$ split by $(A_1, B_1)$ is included in $ins(F_2) \setminus U(F_2)$, and so $U(F_1) \cap ins(F_2) = U(F_1) \cap U(F_2)$. Similarly, $U(F_2) \cap ins(F_1) = U(F_1) \cap U(F_2)$, and so $ins(F_1) \cap ins(F_2) = U(F_1) \cap U(F_2)$, as required. $\square$

If $\mathscr{T}$ is a tangle in $\Gamma$, a separation $(A, B) \in \mathscr{T}$ is said to be $\lambda$-\textit{linked to} $\mathscr{T}$ if there is no $(A', B') \in \mathscr{T}$ of smaller $\lambda$-order with $A \subseteq A'$ and $B' \subseteq B$. If $F$ is a circuit of $\Gamma^*$ with $\frac{1}{2} |E(F)| < \min(ord(\mathscr{T}), rep(\Gamma))$, let $\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2$ be the closures of the two components of $\hat{\Sigma} \setminus U(F)$; then $\mathscr{T}$ contains one of $(\Gamma \cap \Sigma_1, \Gamma \cap \Sigma_2)$, $(\Gamma \cap \Sigma_2, \Gamma \cap \Sigma_1)$, and if that separation is $\lambda$-linked to $\mathscr{T}$ we say that $F$ is $\lambda$-\textit{linked (to} $\mathscr{T}$\textit{)}.
